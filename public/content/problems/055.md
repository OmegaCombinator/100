## Formalization

This is a complete Acorn proof of the product of segments of chords theorem for
two intersecting chords of a circle in the real coordinate plane. The public Top
100 wrapper states:

```acorn
theorem top100_055_product_of_segments_of_chords(
    center: Point2[Real], radius_sq: Real,
    q: Point2[Real], r: Point2[Real], s: Point2[Real], t: Point2[Real], x: Point2[Real],
    u: Real, v: Real,
    qx: Real, rx: Real, sx: Real, tx: Real
) {
    center.on_circle(radius_sq, q) and
    center.on_circle(radius_sq, r) and
    center.on_circle(radius_sq, s) and
    center.on_circle(radius_sq, t) and
    Real.0 <= u and u <= Real.1 and
    Real.0 <= v and v <= Real.1 and
    x = q.param_line(r, u) and
    x = s.param_line(t, v) and
    point2_real_length_witness(q.sub(x), qx) and
    point2_real_length_witness(r.sub(x), rx) and
    point2_real_length_witness(s.sub(x), sx) and
    point2_real_length_witness(t.sub(x), tx)
    implies qx * rx = sx * tx
}
```

The proof adds reusable `geometry.point2_chord` infrastructure. The main helper
proves the power-of-a-point identity for a parameter point on a chord: after
translating the circle center to the origin, the product of the squared segment
distances is the square of the point's power with respect to the circle. Applying
this to both chords gives equality of squared products.

The Top 100 wrapper then uses nonnegative real length witnesses for the four
segments and the fact that nonnegative real numbers with equal squares are equal,
yielding the usual product-of-lengths statement.

## Interpretation

The formalization uses squared coordinate distance and length witnesses rather
than an explicit square-root function. This matches the classical theorem: if
two chords meet at `x`, then the product of the two segment lengths on one chord
equals the corresponding product on the other chord.

The proof has been verified with Acorn.
