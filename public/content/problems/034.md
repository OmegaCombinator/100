## Formalization

This is a complete Acorn proof that the harmonic-series partial sums do not
converge. Acorn's real-sequence `converges` predicate is the Cauchy condition,
so the proof contradicts the Cauchy bound at radius `1/2`.

The proof uses the upstream harmonic term definition and dyadic block lower
bound from `real.harmonic`. It shows that for every positive index `k`, the
block from `k` to `2k` contributes at least `1/2`; therefore any claimed Cauchy
bound `n` is contradicted by comparing the partial sums at `k = n.suc` and
`2k`.

The final public theorem is:

```acorn
theorem top100_034_divergence_of_harmonic_series {
    not converges(partial[Real](harmonic))
}
```

## Interpretation

This matches the standard theorem that the harmonic series diverges: its
partial sums fail to be Cauchy and hence do not converge.
