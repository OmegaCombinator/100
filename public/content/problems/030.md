## Formalization

This is a complete Acorn proof of the Ballot Problem in binomial-difference
form. The public Top 100 wrapper states:

```acorn
theorem top100_030_the_ballot_problem(positives: Nat, negatives: Nat) {
    negatives <= positives implies
        successful_ballot_words(positives.suc, negatives.suc).length +
            (positives + negatives.suc).binom(positives.suc) =
            (positives + negatives.suc).binom(positives)
}
```

The proof constructs all ballot words with prescribed positive and negative vote
counts, proves their binomial count, and proves uniqueness of the generated
list. It then partitions generated words into weak words and bad-prefix words.

The key combinatorial step is a reflection argument: a bad ballot word with one
extra negative vote is reflected at its first bad prefix, giving a ballot word
with one extra positive vote. The proof also constructs the inverse reflection
and uses uniqueness/permutation lemmas to transfer the count.

## Interpretation

This matches the classical ballot-count formula in an equivalent form that
avoids division: the number of successful ballots is the total binomial count
minus the reflected bad-prefix count.
